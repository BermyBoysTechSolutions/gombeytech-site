AI, in practical terms

The technical pieces, tied to a business task.

These are tools and patterns I work with in hands-on projects. The right choice depends on the task, the data, the provider, and what can be tested—not on how many acronyms fit in a pitch.

Agent orchestration

Break a larger process into well-defined steps or specialist agents, with clear tool permissions and human review where decisions matter.

Knowledge retrieval (RAG)

Find relevant passages in approved documents and use them to ground an answer. Test with known questions, check sources, and abstain when the material does not support an answer.

MCP and tool connections

Connect compatible AI tools to selected business apps. Review the exact permissions and data path; actions that write or send information need separate approval and testing.

Model selection and routing

Compare available models for a task and configure model switching or routing where the chosen software supports it. Provider accounts and usage costs stay under the customer’s control.

AI integrations are scoped to the selected tools and tested workflow. Cloud prompts may be processed by the chosen provider; local inference is claimed only after the full agreed data path is checked.

How it works

A clear plan before a project begins.

You stay in control of the scope, budget, and next step.

1. Free consultation

We discuss what you want to improve, what you already use, and any deadlines or constraints. This first call is for fit and scoping, not a paid technical assessment.

2. Written scope and fixed quote

For work that needs research or careful sizing, I follow up with the deliverables, assumptions, timeline, and fixed project fee. Hardware, subscriptions, travel, and third-party costs are shown separately.

3. Build, test, and hand over

Work begins after you approve the scope. We test the agreed result, review it together, and document what was configured. Any added work is agreed before it is billed.
